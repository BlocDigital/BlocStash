# Golden Hour Prints

Joy has a colour temperature, and it is the last warm hour of the day. This movement treats a photograph as a small, kept thing: a print with a white border, handled, pinned, passed across a table. Nothing is cold or technical. Light is generous, edges are soft, and every surface looks as though it has been in a happy pocket.

The palette is borrowed from the sky between noon and dusk: clear morning blue easing into butter yellow and then peach, set against the layered greens of far hills. One bright sun anchors each composition and is allowed to be the loudest thing present. Hills recede in overlapping bands, each a little deeper than the last, so that distance is felt as warmth fading into green.

Prints tilt a few degrees, as if set down by a hand rather than placed by a grid. Their borders are thick and bright, their shadows low and kind, and their handwritten captions are brief enough to be a whisper. Space is generous and the composition breathes. Every curve, overlap and shadow is the product of meticulous, painstaking craft by someone at the top of their field, and should look labored over for countless hours while appearing effortless.

Text appears only as a quiet caption. The image carries the feeling.
