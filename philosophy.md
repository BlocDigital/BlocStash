# Cyanotype Meridian

Light, fixed once and left to develop. The movement treats an image as evidence of a moment: a single exposure that has been indexed, framed and shelved with the patience of an archivist. Blue is not a mood here but a chemistry. Prussian blue and bone paper carry the whole palette, with a lone point of warm yellow as the only mark of a place on the earth.

Space is organised like a contact sheet: ranks of identical frames, each a different density of exposure, held in a rigorous grid. Over this quiet field a wireframe of meridians passes, thin as a scratch on a negative, so that every frame feels addressed to a coordinate. Repetition builds meaning; the eye is rewarded for staying with it. Every spacing and stroke is the product of painstaking, master-level craft, calibrated by someone who has spent thousands of hours at the enlarger.

Typography is a whisper: small monospaced registration marks in the margins, as if stamped by the lab. Nothing shouts and nothing overlaps. The one saturated gesture is the single yellow point, which makes the composition legible as a map of where the light fell. The work should look meticulously laboured over, the result of deep expertise and countless refinements.
